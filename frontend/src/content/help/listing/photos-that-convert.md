Tenants decide in about four seconds whether to open your listing. Photos are that decision.

## The shot list

You need at least three photos to publish, and the platform default limit is ten (it can be set between 3 and 20; the wizard shows the current limit before you upload). Aim for at least five, and take eight if the limit allows it. You label each photo by room; to publish a home, at least two of living room, kitchen, bedroom and bathroom need a photo. Commercial and land listings have their own key categories. Your first photo is the cover. Photos can be JPEG, PNG or HEIC, and large ones are compressed for you. Changing photos on a live listing sends it for a quick re-check.

1. **Living room**, from the doorway corner, shooting into the room
2. **Kitchen**, showing counter length and storage
3. **Each bedroom**, from the door corner
4. **Each bathroom**
5. **Balcony**, and the view from it
6. **Building exterior** and entrance
7. **Amenities** actually used — lift lobby, parking, garden
8. **Floor plan**, if you have one

## Technique

- **Shoot in daylight.** Mid-morning, all curtains open, all lights also on. Never at night.
- **Shoot from a corner** at chest height. Corners make rooms read as their real size; centre shots flatten them.
- **Hold the phone level.** Tilted verticals are the single most common flaw and they look amateur.
- **Landscape orientation.** It matches the listing card and the gallery.
- **Wipe the lens.** More listings are ruined by a smudged lens than by a bad camera.

## Before you shoot

- Clear all clutter, personal items, drying clothes and shoes
- Open curtains, switch on lights and fans
- Close toilet lids, clear kitchen counters
- Fix the obvious — a burnt bulb or a peeling switchboard photographs badly and reads as neglect

> [!WARNING]
> Do not use stock images, renders, or photos of a different unit. Reviewers reject photos that do not look real for the listing, and tenants who travel across Pune to find a different flat do not rent it.

## What hurts a listing

- Fewer than five photos
- Photos taken at night or with the lights off
- Heavy filters or beautification
- No exterior shot — it reads as hiding something
- Watermarks from another portal

## Video and reels

You can add a YouTube walkthrough link. Shoot it slowly, hallway to room, in the order someone would actually walk. Changing the video on a live listing sends it for a quick re-check.
