## Start with comparables, not with your target

Open the [locality page](/locality) for your area and look at what is currently listed at your configuration and carpet area. Then narrow further:

- Same society, if there are listings
- Same floor band — top floor and ground floor both discount
- Same furnishing level
- Similar age and condition

The number that matters is what comparable units **rented for**, not what they were listed at. Listings that have been up for six weeks are telling you what does not work.

## Adjust for what you actually have

| Factor | Typical effect |
| --- | --- |
| Covered parking | +3–5% |
| Fully furnished | +15–25% over unfurnished |
| Top floor without lift | −5–10% |
| East / north-east facing | +2–4% |
| Society with gym and pool | +5–8% |
| Main-road facing (noise) | −3–5% |

## The cost of overpricing

> [!IMPORTANT]
> An overpriced listing does not simply take longer to rent — it goes stale. Fewer enquiries means lower ranking, which means fewer views, which compounds. Two months vacant at ₹30,000 costs ₹60,000; renting at ₹28,000 instead costs ₹24,000 over a year.

If you have had fewer than five enquiries in two weeks, the price is the problem, not the market.

## Deposit as a lever

Deposit is often more negotiable than rent, and tenants feel it more sharply. Offering two months' deposit instead of three in a market where three is standard will widen your applicant pool more than a ₹1,000 rent reduction.

## Escalation

5–8% annually is standard and accepted. Above 10% invites the tenant to move at renewal, and turnover costs you a vacancy period plus new-tenant effort.
