To rent out a flat in Pune, set a market rent, get the flat ready, tell your society, screen tenants, agree the terms in writing, register a leave and licence agreement, submit your tenant's details to the police and hand over with a signed inventory. Registration and police verification are legal duties that fall on you as the owner.

## Key facts

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Agreement | Must be a registered leave and licence agreement, even for 11 months; the landlord is responsible (Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999, section 55) |
| Registration cost | Stamp duty of 0.25% of the agreement's value plus a ₹1,000 registration fee in PMC or PCMC |
| Police | Submit tenant details separately from registering the agreement |
| Society charge | A cooperative society's non-occupancy charge is capped at 10% of service charges under a 2001 Maharashtra government resolution |
| Tax on rent | Taxable as income from house property, with a 30% standard deduction after municipal tax |
| TDS | An individual tenant paying more than ₹50,000 a month deducts 2% TDS |

Work through this checklist from top to bottom. Renting out a flat is not complicated, but the order matters. Owners who skip a step usually pay for it later: a flat that sits empty for months, a tenant who stops paying, or a penalty for an unregistered agreement.

## 1. Set a rent the market will accept

Look at what similar flats in your society and locality are listed for: same BHK, a similar floor, the same furnishing and parking. You can compare [flats for rent in Pune](/listings?deal=rent) by locality and budget, and see cited ranges in our [Pune rent report 2026](/blog/pune-rent-report-2026). To judge what the flat earns against its price, try the [rental yield calculator](/tools/rental-yield-calculator).

Pricing a little high feels harmless, but empty months cost more than a slightly lower rent. A flat at ₹30,000 that stays vacant for two months loses ₹60,000. Pricing it ₹2,000 lower costs only ₹22,000 over an 11-month agreement.

Decide the furnishing level before you list:

- **Unfurnished:** fans, lights and a basic kitchen.
- **Semi-furnished:** adds wardrobes, a modular kitchen and often a geyser and curtains.
- **Fully furnished:** adds beds, a sofa, a fridge, a washing machine and so on.

## 2. Get the flat ready

Tenants decide in the first minute of a visit. Fix leaking taps and seepage, service the geyser and any air conditioners, repaint if the walls are marked and get a deep clean done.

Take photos in daylight with the lights on, one or two per room, plus the view, the parking and the society amenities. Our guide to [photos that get more calls](/help/a/photos-that-convert) shows what works.

## 3. Check your society's rules

Tell your housing society office before the tenant moves in. Most Pune societies ask for the tenant's details and a copy of the registered agreement. Ask the secretary for the society's process so move-in day is not held up at the gate. See [society NOC for tenants in Pune](/blog/society-noc-for-tenants-pune).

A cooperative housing society can charge a non-occupancy charge on a flat that is let out. Under a 2001 Maharashtra government resolution, it cannot be more than **10% of the service charges** (excluding municipal taxes), and it does not apply if a close relative lives in the flat.

## 4. List it and screen tenants

[List your flat on Draazy](/list-property) for free. Tenants call you directly, so there is no broker and no brokerage on either side.

When people call, meet them in person before you agree to anything. Useful questions:

- Where do they work, and how many people will live in the flat?
- When do they want to move in, and how long do they plan to stay?
- Can they share an ID and, for working professionals, an employee ID or offer letter?
- Can you speak to their current landlord?

More in [tenant screening for owners in Pune](/blog/tenant-screening-owners-pune).

## 5. Agree the terms in writing

Most disputes come from terms nobody wrote down. Agree these before anyone signs:

- **Rent and the due date,** and how it will be paid.
- **Security deposit,** and how soon it will be refunded after move-out. Read our guide to [security deposits in Pune](/blog/security-deposit-pune).
- **Lock-in period** and **notice period.** See [notice period and lock-in clauses](/blog/notice-period-lock-in-rent-agreement).
- **Annual escalation,** usually 5–10%.
- **Who pays what:** society maintenance, electricity, water, piped gas and minor repairs. See [who pays maintenance charges](/blog/maintenance-charges-who-pays-pune).
- **House rules** on pets, visitors or subletting, if they matter to you.

## 6. Register the agreement

In Maharashtra every leave and licence agreement must be registered, including an 11-month agreement, and the legal duty sits with you as the owner. It can be done fully online. Our guide to [rent agreements in Pune](/blog/rent-agreement-pune) explains stamp duty and the documents, the [rent agreement cost calculator](/tools/rent-agreement-cost-pune) gives the fees for your rent and deposit, or [Draazy can draft and register it for you](/services/rent-agreement). If you are unsure which document you need, read [leave and licence vs lease](/blog/leave-and-licence-vs-lease).

## 7. Complete police verification

Submit your tenant's details to the police before they move in. It is a separate step from registering the agreement. See our guide to [tenant police verification in Pune](/blog/tenant-police-verification-pune).

## 8. Hand over properly

On the day you hand over the keys:

- Walk through the flat together with an inventory of furniture and fittings, and take dated photos.
- Note the electricity and gas meter readings.
- Record how many sets of keys and access cards you hand over.
- Get the inventory signed by both of you and keep a copy each.

This one page is what settles deposit arguments when the tenant moves out.

## 9. Know the tax basics

Rent is taxable as income from house property. You can deduct the municipal property tax you paid, and then claim a standard deduction of 30% for repairs and upkeep, whatever you actually spent. This continues under the Income-tax Act, 2025, in force from 1 April 2026. Interest on a home loan for the flat is also deductible, within limits. See [rental income tax for owners](/blog/rental-income-tax-owners) for the detail.

If your rent is more than ₹50,000 a month, an individual tenant must deduct TDS at 2% and give you a certificate for it. Speak to your CA about your own situation.

> [!TIP]
> Ask for rent by bank transfer or UPI rather than cash. It gives both of you a clean record for tax and for any dispute. Tenants who claim HRA need rent receipts, which the [rent receipt generator](/tools/rent-receipt-generator) makes.
