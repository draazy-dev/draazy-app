Do not transfer any money before you have seen the flat and met the owner in person. Most rental scams in Pune follow one script: a flat priced too low, an owner who cannot meet you, and an urgent request for a token, "booking charge" or refundable fee. If you have already paid, call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in straight away.

## The scripts scammers use

### The owner who is "out of town"

The owner says they are posted elsewhere, often claiming to be in the army or a central government job, and cannot show the flat. They promise to courier the keys once you pay the deposit or a token. There are no keys. Usually the flat is not theirs, or it does not exist.

### The urgent token

"Three other families are interested. Pay ₹5,000 today and I will hold it for you." A genuine owner will hold a flat for a day so you can visit. They will not insist on a UPI transfer from a stranger.

### The "refundable" visit or gate-pass fee

You are asked to pay a small amount to "confirm the visit", to get a gate pass for the society, or to "verify" that you are serious. The fee is supposedly refundable. It never is, and the next demand is always larger.

### The QR code that takes money

The other person sends a QR code and says that scanning it will *send you* a refund or advance. Scanning a QR code and entering your UPI PIN only ever takes money out of your account. You never need your PIN to receive money.

### The photo switch

The listing shows a bright, renovated flat. On site, you are told "that one just went, but this one is available", and the flat on offer is worse and more expensive. This is not always fraud, but it is a sign the listing was made to get calls, not to rent that flat.

## How to check a flat and its owner

Before you pay anything, take these steps. A genuine owner will not mind any of them.

1. **Visit in person.** See the flat, check that the keys work and look at the water supply, the electricity meter and the parking.
2. **Meet the owner, not only a "caretaker".** If someone else shows the flat, speak to the owner on a video call during the visit and match their name to their ID.
3. **Match the name to the property.** Ask to see the Index II or the sale deed. A recent property tax bill or society maintenance receipt in the same name is a good cross-check. Our guide explains [what an Index II shows](/blog/index-2-pune-property).
4. **Ask the society office.** The secretary or the security desk usually knows who owns the flat and whether it is genuinely up for rent.
5. **Pay only against a written receipt,** preferably by bank transfer to an account in the owner's name. Avoid cash.
6. **Keep the token small and conditional.** Write down that it will be adjusted against the deposit and refunded if the agreement is not signed.

> [!TIP]
> Do a reverse image search on listing photos that look too polished. If the same photos appear on several sites under different names or localities, walk away.

## Too good to be true usually is

Scam listings are priced to stand out. A 2 BHK in Baner or Kharadi listed at half the going rent is not a lucky find. ## If you have already paid

Act quickly. The first few hours matter.

- **Call 1930**, the national cyber crime helpline, or report it at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in). Quick reports give the best chance of freezing the money in transit.
- **Inform your bank** and ask them to raise a fraud complaint on the transaction.
- **Keep the evidence:** screenshots of the listing, the chat, phone numbers, UPI IDs and transaction references.
- **File a complaint** at your nearest police station or the cyber police.
