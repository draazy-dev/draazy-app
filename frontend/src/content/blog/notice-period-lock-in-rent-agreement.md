A lock-in is the period in which neither side can end the agreement without a penalty. A notice period is how much warning one side must give before leaving or asking the other to leave. Maharashtra law does not fix either number for a leave and licence agreement, so the registered agreement decides. Write both down, with the penalty for breaking them.

> This is general information, not legal advice. Have a lawyer read any agreement with unusual terms.

## What is a lock-in period?

A lock-in is a stretch at the start of the agreement, often 3 or 6 months in an 11-month term, during which neither side can leave without a consequence. It protects the owner from a quick vacancy and protects the tenant from a sudden demand to leave.

The usual consequence for a tenant who leaves early is losing the deposit, or paying the rent for the rest of the lock-in. For an owner who asks the tenant to leave early, a fair clause is to refund the deposit in full and pay a month's rent as compensation.

Write the consequence as a number or a formula. "Penalty as per law" tells you nothing.

## What is a notice period?

Notice is the written warning before the agreement ends early, after the lock-in. One month and two months are the usual choices. A shorter notice suits tenants who may relocate for work. A longer one suits owners who need time to find a new tenant.

Agree the following in the clause:

- **Who can give notice.** Both sides, with the same period. A clause that lets only the owner end the agreement is lopsided.
- **How it is given.** Written, by email or post to the addresses in the agreement.
- **When it starts.** From the day the other side receives it.
- **Rent for the notice period.** The tenant pays rent until the last day. They should not be asked to pay extra, unless the notice is short.

## Can a tenant adjust the deposit against notice rent?

Many tenants try to skip the last month's rent and let the deposit cover it. Owners often object, because the deposit is meant to cover damage and unpaid bills. Say in the agreement whether adjusting is allowed. If it is not, the tenant pays the last month in full and gets the whole deposit back after handover. ## What happens when the term ends?

Under section 24 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, a licensee must hand over the flat when the licence period ends. If they do not, the owner can apply to the Competent Authority for eviction, and the licensee can be liable for damages at double the agreed licence fee until they leave. A written agreement counts as conclusive evidence of its terms, which is another reason to keep the details accurate.

The owner cannot take matters into their own hands. Under section 29, a landlord may not cut off essential supplies such as water or electricity without just cause. Changing the lock or switching off the supply does not end a dispute; it starts one.

If both sides want to continue, sign a fresh registered agreement or a registered renewal. A verbal "continue" does not extend a registered term. ## Clauses to include

Copy the structure below into your agreement and fill in the numbers:

1. **Term.** Start and end dates, for example 11 months.
2. **Lock-in.** Number of months, and what each side owes if they leave inside it.
3. **Notice.** Number of days or months, in writing, from either side.
4. **Handover.** Condition of the flat at exit, and the date of the final inspection.
5. **Deposit refund.** Number of days after handover, and what can be deducted.
6. **Rent increase on renewal.** A percentage, so there is no argument later.
7. **Early exit by the owner.** Compensation and a refund timeline.

## Before you sign

Read the lock-in and notice clauses twice. If you are a tenant who may change jobs within a year, ask for a shorter lock-in. If you are an owner who needs the flat for family, say so and agree a notice period that gives you time. Terms that both sides understood at signing are the ones that survive a disagreement.
