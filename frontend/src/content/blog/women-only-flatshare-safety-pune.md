Before you pay for a flatshare or PG in Pune, verify who runs it, see the exact room in daylight, bring someone with you, and read the written terms. Save 112 and 181 in your phone, and tell a friend where you are going. Do not pay a token for a place you have not seen.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In an emergency, call 112. This guide is general advice, not a guarantee of safety. Trust your own judgement and leave if something feels wrong.

## Who should I verify before I pay?

Start with the person who offers you the room.

- **Ask who the owner is** and see the ownership proof or the latest property tax or electricity bill. If someone says they are the owner's "friend" or "manager", speak to the owner.
- **Check the person matches the listing.** Names on the agreement, the bank account and the ID should be consistent.
- **Ask about police verification.** A genuine owner or PG operator knows that residents' details go to the police. See [tenant police verification in Pune](/blog/tenant-police-verification-pune).
- **Look the PG up.** Ask for its address, the operator's name and a landline or office. Speak to a current resident if you can.
- **Search the name and number online** for complaints.

## How should I visit the place?

1. **Visit in daylight**, and then once more in the evening if you can. Notice how the street and the building feel after dark.
2. **Take someone with you** on the first visit, or share your live location with a friend.
3. **See the exact room** you will get, not a similar one.
4. **Walk the route** to the nearest bus stop, metro or auto stand. Check street lighting.
5. **Check the building:** security guard, CCTV in common areas, a working lift, and a lock on your room door.
6. **Meet the flatmates** or other residents. Ask how long each has lived there.
7. **Ask about house rules:** guests, entry times, and who has keys to your room.

## What should I check in a flatshare?

- **Who else lives there.** Ask who each flatmate is, what they do and how long they plan to stay.
- **Whether guests are allowed overnight**, and who decides.
- **Your own lockable room.** It should have a lock you control.
- **How the agreement is set up.** Make sure your name is on the agreement or a written understanding. Our [flatmate agreement guide](/blog/flatmate-agreement-pune) shows how.
- **Who has copies of the keys.**

## What should be in writing for a PG?

Ask for a written agreement or at least a receipt showing:

- Monthly rent, deposit and the date and mode of payment
- What is included: food, Wi-Fi, laundry, electricity
- Notice period and when the deposit is returned
- Entry and visitor rules

Our [PG or flat share comparison](/blog/pg-vs-flat-share-pune) covers deposit and paperwork. Women's PGs often have rules on entry times and visitors; read them and decide whether they suit you.

## Which payment habits protect me?

- **Pay by bank transfer or UPI to a named account**, not to a stranger's personal wallet.
- **Never pay a token before seeing the room.** Asking for a token before you have seen the room is a classic rental scam. See [rental scams in Pune](/blog/rental-scams-pune).
- **Get a receipt for every payment**, with the date and what it covers.
- **Do not share your OTP, card details or an unnecessary copy of your Aadhaar** before you have decided.

## Which helplines should I save?

| Number | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 112 | Police, fire, ambulance | National emergency number |
| 181 | Women Helpline | Support and information for women in distress |
| 1091 | Women's helpline in Pune | Operated through Pune Police's Bharosa Cell, per Indian Express (2021); numbers can change, so confirm |

For harassment at home or from a flatmate, a landlord or a PG operator, call the police. The Pune City Police website, [punepolice.gov.in](https://punepolice.gov.in), lists current contacts. The Bharosa Cell, set up in 2019, offers counselling and legal help for women, and the Damini Squad patrols public places.

## What if something feels wrong?

Leave. You do not owe anyone a reason. If you have already paid, keep your messages and receipts, and report to the police if you suspect fraud or threats. Tell a friend or family member early; do not wait until it feels serious.
