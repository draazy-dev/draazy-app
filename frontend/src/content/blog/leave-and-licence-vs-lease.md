A lease transfers a right to enjoy the property to the tenant. A leave and licence only gives permission to occupy it. Most Pune home rentals use a leave and licence because, under section 24 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999, the owner can recover the flat when the licence period ends by applying to the Competent Authority.

Whatever you call it, section 55 of the same Act requires the agreement to be in writing and registered.

> This is general information, not legal advice. Have a lawyer check any agreement that is out of the ordinary.

## Key facts

| Item | Lease | Leave and licence |
|---|---|---|
| Legal nature | Transfer of a right to enjoy the property for a time, for rent or other consideration (Transfer of Property Act 1882, section 105) | A right to do something on the owner's property that does not amount to an easement or an interest in it (Indian Easements Act 1882, section 52) |
| Registration in Maharashtra | In writing and registered (Maharashtra Rent Control Act 1999, section 55) | In writing and registered (same section) |
| Recovering the flat | Governed by the Act's other provisions and the agreement; take legal advice | On expiry, the owner applies to the Competent Authority, which orders eviction once it is satisfied the period has expired (section 24) |
| If the licensee stays on | Not covered here | Damages at double the licence fee until dispossessed (section 24(2)) |

## What is a lease?

Section 105 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 defines a lease of immovable property as a transfer of a right to enjoy the property, made for a certain time, in return for a price paid or promised. The owner is the lessor and the tenant is the lessee. The lessee gets a right in the property for the period, not just permission to use it.

## What is a leave and licence?

Section 52 of the Indian Easements Act, 1882 describes a licence. It is a right given by the owner to do something on the owner's property that would otherwise be unlawful, which does not amount to an easement or an interest in the property.

In everyday terms, the owner gives the licensee permission to live in the flat for a period and a fee. The licence fee is what most people call rent. The licensee does not become a tenant.

## Why do Pune owners use leave and licence?

Section 24 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 says a licensee in occupation of premises given on licence for residence must hand back possession on expiry of the licence period. If they do not, the landlord can apply to the Competent Authority, which must pass an order for eviction once satisfied that the period has expired. A licensee who stays on pays damages at double the licence fee fixed in the agreement. The section also says a written agreement of licence is conclusive evidence of the facts stated in it.

This clear route to getting the flat back when the period ends is a main reason the leave and licence, usually for 11 months, is the common form for home rentals in Pune.

## Does a leave and licence have to be registered?

Yes. Section 55 says any agreement for leave and licence or letting, made after the Act began, must be in writing and registered under the Registration Act, 1908. The responsibility for registering is on the landlord. Without a written, registered agreement, the tenant's account of the terms prevails unless proved otherwise. A landlord who breaks the rule can be punished with imprisonment of up to three months, a fine of up to ₹5,000, or both.

Notarising the agreement is not registration. Read [rent agreements in Pune](/blog/rent-agreement-pune) for the process, which can be completed online.

## What does registration cost?

Our [rent agreement guide](/blog/rent-agreement-pune) explains how stamp duty on a leave and licence is worked out: 0.25% of the agreement value, which counts the rent for the term plus notional interest on any refundable deposit, and a registration fee on top. Duty for a document called a lease may be worked out differently, so get the exact figure from IGR or a lawyer before you choose.

## What does it mean for the tenant?

- **You have the right to occupy** for the licence period on the agreed terms. The agreement is the main protection you have, so read it fully.
- **Insist on registration.** It is the owner's duty, and it helps you if there is a dispute about the terms or the deposit.
- **Know the exit terms.** Check the lock-in and notice period, and how soon the deposit is returned. See [notice period and lock-in](/blog/notice-period-lock-in-rent-agreement) and [security deposits](/blog/security-deposit-pune).
- **Keep rent receipts.** Use the [rent receipt generator](/tools/rent-receipt-generator) if you need them for HRA.

## What does it mean for the owner?

- **Use a proper leave and licence agreement,** with a fixed period and clear terms. Name and substance should match: a document labelled as licence that actually gives a tenant's rights may not be treated as one. A lawyer can draft it for you.
- **Register it,** and complete [police verification](/blog/tenant-police-verification-pune).
- **Plan the renewal date.** The licence ends on expiry. Renew in writing and register the new agreement.
- **Understand the tax side.** Read [tax on rental income](/blog/rental-income-tax-owners).
