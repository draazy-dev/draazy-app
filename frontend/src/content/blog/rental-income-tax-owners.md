Rent you receive on a flat is taxed under the head "income from house property". You subtract the municipal tax you paid, then a flat 30% standard deduction on what is left, and you can also deduct interest on a loan taken for the property. What remains is added to your other income and taxed at your slab rate.

This guide covers the basics for the tax year 2026-27, which falls under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general information, not tax advice. Rules, forms and section numbers change, and your situation may differ. Speak to a chartered accountant (CA) before you file.

## Key facts

| Item | What applies | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Head of income | Income from house property | Income-tax Act 2025, sections 20 to 22 |
| Municipal taxes | Deducted from the annual value, if actually paid by the owner during the tax year | Income-tax Act 2025, section 21(3) |
| Standard deduction | 30% of the annual value, whatever you actually spent | Income-tax Act 2025, section 22(1)(a) |
| Loan interest | Deductible for a property bought, built or repaired with a loan | Income-tax Act 2025, section 22(1)(b) |
| TDS on rent | Section 393(1) of the 2025 Act (earlier section 194-IB); reported on Form 141, Schedule A | Income Tax Department, Form 141 FAQs |

## How is rental income taxed in India?

The law starts with the "annual value" of the flat. For a flat you let out, this is the higher of two figures: the rent it might reasonably be expected to fetch, or the actual rent you receive or are due to receive (section 21(1)). If the flat was vacant for part of the year and you received less because of it, the rent actually received is used (section 21(2)). Rent you could not recover is left out, subject to the rules (section 21(4)).

From the annual value you deduct:

1. **Municipal taxes** levied by a local authority, such as property tax to the PMC or PCMC, that you actually paid during the year. Tax paid by the tenant does not count. Section 21(3).
2. **A 30% standard deduction** on the annual value as determined under section 21. It covers repairs and upkeep and you get it without showing bills. Section 22(1)(a).
3. **Interest on a home loan** taken to buy, build or repair the property (section 22(1)(b)).

## A worked example

These are illustrative figures, not tax advice.

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent: ₹25,000 a month for 12 months | ₹3,00,000 |
| Less property tax paid by you | ₹6,000 |
| Annual value after municipal tax | ₹2,94,000 |
| Less 30% standard deduction | ₹88,200 |
| **Income from house property** (before loan interest) | **₹2,05,800** |

If you pay interest on a loan for this flat, you can deduct it too, subject to the limits in the Act. Ask your CA how it applies to you, especially if the interest is more than the income.

## When does a tenant deduct TDS on rent?

If your tenant is an individual or Hindu undivided family (HUF) who is not liable to a tax audit, and pays you more than ₹50,000 a month, they must deduct tax at source at 2%. Earlier this was section 194-IB. In the 2025 Act it sits in section 393(1), Table serial 2(i).

How it works:

- **The tenant does not need a TAN.** They report and pay through Form 141, Schedule A, using their PAN login on the income tax e-filing portal.
- **They deduct once a year,** at the end of the tax year or at the end of the tenancy, whichever comes first. On the form they choose "end of tax year" or "end of tenancy" and enter the total rent for the year and the rent for the last month.
- **They pay within 30 days** from the end of the month in which they deduct, and file Form 141 within the same time.
- **You get a TDS certificate,** which the tenant downloads from the TDS-TRACES portal.

Give your tenant your PAN when you sign the agreement. Then check that the TDS shows in your annual tax statement before you file, so you can claim the credit.

If your tenant is a company or an audited business, other TDS rules apply and the tenant will use a TAN. Check with your CA.
