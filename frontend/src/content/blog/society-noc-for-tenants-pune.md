Many Pune housing societies ask for a "tenant NOC" before a tenant moves in. The society's dealings are with the flat owner, who is the member, not with you. What the rules clearly limit is the money: non-occupancy charges on a rented flat are capped at 10% of the flat's service charges. Ask for paperwork requests in writing, and ask which bye-law they come from.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general information, not legal advice. Each society also has its own registered bye-laws. Read yours, or ask the owner for a copy.

## Does a tenant need a society NOC?

In practice, yes: most societies expect to be told about a tenant, and many ask for a letter, often called an NOC, before the tenant moves in. Whether a society can refuse to issue one depends on its bye-laws and on the reason it gives. We could not confirm a single rule that lets a society ban letting altogether, so do not assume it can.

Practical advice for both sides: start the NOC request when the agreement is being drafted, not on moving day. Owners should ask the society for its letting procedure and any form in writing.

## What can a society usually ask for?

Requests that are common and reasonable:

- A copy of the registered leave and licence agreement
- Tenant's photo ID and address proof, plus the owner's contact details
- Names of everyone who will live in the flat
- Proof that tenant details have been given to the police
- Vehicle details, if you want a parking sticker

These requests help the society keep a record of who lives in the building. Our guide to [tenant police verification in Pune](/blog/tenant-police-verification-pune) explains the police step, and the [rent agreement guide](/blog/rent-agreement-pune) covers registration.

## What does a society charge for a rented flat?

Societies charge the owner a non-occupancy charge when a flat is let rather than lived in by the owner. Under the amended Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Rules, in force since 30 June 2026, that charge cannot be more than 10% of the flat's service charges. The cap is not new: the state set it by an order in 2001, and courts have upheld it. The Rules now carry it.

Two points follow:

- The charge is levied on the owner. If an owner passes it on to you, it should be written into the agreement, along with the amount.
- A society cannot, on its own, add a "NOC fee", "tenant fee" or "move-in penalty" that the rules do not provide for. If one is demanded, ask for the bye-law or resolution, and the receipt.

Some societies also ask for a refundable move-in or lift deposit. Ask what it is for, how much it is, and when it is returned. Get a receipt in the owner's or society's name.

## Can a society stop you using the amenities?

A tenant lives in the flat under the owner's right, so ordinary services such as water, lift and parking allotted to the flat are part of the arrangement. Disagreements usually come from unpaid dues or breaches of society rules. Do not accept a verbal "you cannot use the lift or the clubhouse". Ask for it in writing and raise it with the owner, who is the member and can take it up with the managing committee.

Ask the owner to take the matter up with the managing committee, and to approach the Deputy Registrar of co-operative societies if the society acts beyond the rules.

## What if the society refuses or delays?

1. **Ask for the reason in writing.** A written refusal lets the owner challenge it.
2. **Ask which bye-law or rule it relies on.**
3. **Let the owner lead.** The owner is the member and the right person to deal with the committee.
4. **Do not pay a charge without a receipt.**
5. **Do not move in on a promise.** If the NOC is still open, agree with the owner when you will move in and when rent starts.

## Before you move in

Keep a short folder: your agreement, ID, police acknowledgement, NOC letter if one was given, and receipts for any society payment. If a dispute starts, these are the papers that settle it.
