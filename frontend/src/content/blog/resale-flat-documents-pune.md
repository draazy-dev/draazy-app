For a resale flat, check that the seller can legally sell it, that the papers trace back to the builder and that nothing is owed on it. The core set is the chain of registered agreements, an Index II for each, the society's papers, the land record and the latest tax and dues receipts.

Do this before you pay more than a token amount. Ask a lawyer to review the originals or certified copies.

## Key facts

| Item | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| How ownership passes | A sale of immovable property worth ₹100 or more is valid only by a registered instrument | Transfer of Property Act 1882, section 54 |
| Index II | A one-page extract of a registered document, searchable free on IGR Maharashtra | IGR Maharashtra, October 2026 |
| TDS on property purchase | Reported through Form 141, Schedule B, on the Income Tax e-filing portal | Income Tax Department, Form 141 FAQs, October 2026 |
| Occupancy certificate | Issued by the competent authority to permit occupation of a building | RERA Act 2016, section 2(zf) |

## What documents show the seller owns the flat?

1. **The chain of agreements.** Ask for every registered document from the builder's first agreement to the seller, in order. Each one should show the previous owner as the seller. A gap is a red flag.
2. **The seller's registered agreement or sale deed.** An unregistered agreement does not pass ownership. Section 54 of the Transfer of Property Act requires a registered instrument for a sale of this kind.
3. **Index II for each registered document.** It shows the price, the parties, the property and the registration details, and you can match it against the copies you were given. Read our guide to [Index II](/blog/index-2-pune-property) for how to search and read it.
4. **Identity of the seller.** The name on the papers should match the seller's PAN and Aadhaar. If the owner is not the person selling, or a power of attorney is involved, get legal advice first.
5. **If the owner has died,** ask for the papers that show who the legal heirs are and that all of them agree to the sale.

## What should the society provide?

- **Share certificate.** In a cooperative housing society, a flat owner is a member holding shares. The certificate should be in the seller's name and match the flat number.
- **Society NOC for the sale.** Many societies and banks ask for it. Ask the society about its process and charges for transferring membership.
- **No-dues certificate.** It confirms that maintenance, sinking fund and other society charges are paid up to date.
- **Society registration details and the conveyance.** Ask whether the builder has conveyed the land and building to the society. Unsold conveyance can create problems later.

Our [maintenance charges guide](/blog/maintenance-charges-who-pays-pune) explains the charges you will pay as an owner.

## Which land and building records should I see?

- **Land record.** Depending on the location, the land record is a 7/12 extract (satbara) or a property card from the city survey. Ask your lawyer which applies to the plot, Check the owner's name and any encumbrance or other rights noted.
- **Approved plan and occupancy certificate.** The flat should be in a building with an occupancy certificate. See our guide to [OC and CC](/blog/oc-cc-check-pune).
- **MahaRERA registration,** if the project was registered. See [how to check it](/blog/check-maharera-registration).

## Which receipts and dues should I check?

- **Property tax receipt** from the PMC or PCMC, for the latest year, in the seller's name.
- **Electricity bill** and, where relevant, water and piped gas bills. Confirm no unpaid amounts and that the meter is in the flat's name.
- **Society maintenance receipts** for the recent months.

Ask the seller to clear any dues before the sale, or agree in writing who will pay them.

## What if the flat has a loan on it?

If the seller has a home loan, the bank holds the original documents. Ask for the bank's statement of the outstanding amount and its letter agreeing to release the flat when the loan is repaid. Plan the payments so the loan is cleared and the documents are released at the same time as you pay. A lawyer can set up the steps.

## What happens on the buyer's side?

- **Home loan.** Banks run their own legal check on these papers. See our [home loan documents checklist](/blog/home-loan-documents-pune) and [eligibility guide](/blog/home-loan-eligibility-pune).
- **Stamp duty and registration.** You pay these on the sale registration. Use the [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator-maharashtra) or read our [stamp duty guide](/blog/stamp-duty-flat-pune).
- **TDS.** The buyer may have to deduct tax when paying the seller, and report it through Form 141, Schedule B, with PAN login on the income tax e-filing portal. Ask your CA about the threshold and rate that apply to your purchase.
