Screen a tenant on three things: who they are, whether they can pay and how they have behaved as a tenant before. Meet them, check their documents, speak to a previous landlord and complete police verification. Then apply the same questions to every applicant.

Screening protects you from bad tenants. It does not give you licence to turn people down for who they are.

## What should I ask a tenant before showing the flat?

A short conversation on the phone saves wasted visits. Ask:

- Where do they work, and how many people will live in the flat?
- When do they want to move in, and for how long?
- Do they have pets, or need parking for how many vehicles?
- Can they share a photo ID and an employer ID or offer letter?

Then meet in person at the flat. Our [owner's checklist](/blog/renting-out-flat-pune) covers the full process.

## Which documents can I ask for?

Ask only for what you need to confirm identity and ability to pay:

- **Photo ID and address proof,** such as Aadhaar, passport or driving licence, and PAN.
- **Employment proof:** employer ID, offer letter or recent salary slips. Self-employed tenants can show bank statements or tax returns.
- **A recent photograph** and the details of everyone who will live in the flat.
- **A reference** from the current or previous landlord.

Take copies only for your own records and for the police form. Keep them safe, do not share them and delete them when the tenancy ends. Do not ask for documents you have no use for.

## How do I check income?

Look at whether the rent fits comfortably within what the tenant earns, once their other regular payments are counted. Salary slips and a bank statement showing salary credits are the usual evidence. Decide your own comfort level and apply it to every applicant in the same way.

## How do I check references?

Call the previous landlord yourself, using a number you found independently if possible. Ask:

1. Did they pay on time?
2. Did they leave the flat in good condition and get the deposit back?
3. Did neighbours or the society raise any complaints?
4. Would you rent to them again?

A tenant who is reluctant to give any landlord reference deserves a closer look.

## Do I have to do police verification?

Yes. Owners have to submit the tenant's details to the police before or when the tenant moves in, whether the flat is under Pune City Police or Pimpri Chinchwad Police. Registering the agreement does not replace it. Our guide to [tenant police verification in Pune](/blog/tenant-police-verification-pune) explains the online steps and the documents.

## Do I have to register the agreement?

Yes. In Maharashtra, section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 requires a leave and licence agreement, or an agreement for letting, to be in writing and registered. The duty is on the landlord, and a landlord who does not comply can face imprisonment of up to three months, a fine of up to ₹5,000, or both. Without a registered agreement, the tenant's version of the terms prevails unless the landlord proves otherwise. See [rent agreements in Pune](/blog/rent-agreement-pune) and the [rent agreement cost tool](/tools/rent-agreement-cost-pune).

## What should I not do when screening?

Judge applicants on things that bear on the tenancy: ability to pay, references, number of occupants and how they will treat the flat. Do not refuse someone because of their religion, caste, community, gender identity, marital status or where they come from.

Indian law has no single rule on discrimination in private lettings, but some laws do apply. The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 prohibits denying a transgender person, or treating them unfairly, in the right to rent or occupy property (section 3(g)). Housing society rules may also apply. Beyond the law, a fair process finds better tenants and fewer disputes. Write down your criteria and use the same ones for everyone.

## What else protects me?

- **A clear written agreement** with rent, deposit, lock-in, notice period and house rules. See [notice period and lock-in](/blog/notice-period-lock-in-rent-agreement).
- **A reasonable deposit,** held as the agreement says. Read about [security deposits in Pune](/blog/security-deposit-pune).
- **Payment by bank transfer or UPI,** so there is a record. Tenants can generate receipts with the [rent receipt tool](/tools/rent-receipt-generator).
- **A signed inventory** with dated photos on handover.
- **Beware of scams** on the tenant's side too. Fake payment screenshots and advance-payment requests are common; read our [rental scams guide](/blog/rental-scams-pune).
