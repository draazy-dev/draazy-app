There is no law that says the tenant pays maintenance. In Pune, the common practice is that the tenant pays monthly maintenance and utility bills, while the owner pays property tax, the sinking fund and other one-off society charges. Because it is only custom, write the split into the agreement, line by line.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general information, not legal advice. The "usual split" below is market practice, and you can agree something different.

## Key facts

| Item | Usual split in Pune | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly maintenance (service charges) | Tenant, or included in rent | Market practice |
| Sinking fund, repair and major repair funds | Owner | Market practice |
| Non-occupancy charge | Owner, unless agreed otherwise | Charged to the member; capped at 10% of service charges |
| Property tax | Owner | Market practice |
| Electricity, gas, water meter, internet | Tenant, on actual use | Market practice |

## What is in a society maintenance bill?

A bill from a Pune housing society can combine several heads. The Maharashtra Co-operative Societies (Amendment) Rules, 2026, in force since 30 June 2026, set how some are shared:

- **Service charges** cover staff, housekeeping, security and general upkeep. They are split equally among flats, not by area.
- **Property tax on common areas** follows carpet area.
- **Water charges** depend on the number and size of water inlets.
- **Lift charges** are split among flats in buildings that have a lift.
- **Non-occupancy charges** apply to rented flats and are capped at 10% of service charges.
- **Funds** include the sinking fund and the repair and maintenance fund.

Source: Puranik Associates and PropWatch, 2026. Ask the society for an itemised bill so you know which head is which.

## What is the sinking fund, and who pays?

A sinking fund is money a society sets aside for large repairs and replacements, such as waterproofing or lift renewal. As summarised by PropWatch, the Rules require societies to keep a sinking fund of at least 0.25% a year of each flat's construction cost, and a repair and maintenance fund of at least 0.75% a year on the same basis.

This is the owner's long-term interest in the building, so owners normally pay it and tenants do not. A tenant who pays a bill that includes a sinking fund line, because the society bundles it, can ask the owner to adjust it in the rent.

## Who pays property tax?

Owners usually pay the property tax on the flat to the Pune Municipal Corporation or the Pimpri Chinchwad Municipal Corporation, depending on the area. Tenants do not normally pay it. If a society bill carries a property tax share for the common areas, treat it as the owner's cost unless you agree otherwise.

## Who pays electricity, gas, water and internet?

The person who uses the service pays for it. Electricity is billed by MSEDCL and is normally paid by the tenant on the actual meter reading. Piped gas, internet, DTH and a cook or maid are the tenant's too.

Agree three things:

- **Whose name the connection is in.** If the connection stays in the owner's name, the tenant pays on time and the owner forwards any notice.
- **The meter reading on move-in and move-out.** Photograph both.
- **Pending dues at handover.** The owner clears dues up to move-in; the tenant clears dues up to move-out.

## What if the rent is "inclusive" of maintenance?

Many Pune listings say "maintenance included" or "excluding maintenance". The two can differ by several thousand rupees a month, so ask before you compare. [Rent figures in our 2026 rent report](/blog/pune-rent-report-2026) show what Pune neighbourhoods ask, and you should check whether those include maintenance.

If maintenance is extra, ask the amount in rupees a month and get it in the agreement. If a society raises the charge later, the agreement should say whether the tenant absorbs the increase or only up to a cap.

## How to write it in the agreement

A short table in the agreement, with each item and who pays, removes arguments. Use this layout:

| Item | Paid by | Amount or rule |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly society maintenance | Tenant | ₹ per month, as billed by the society |
| Sinking fund and major repairs | Owner | As billed |
| Non-occupancy charge | Owner | As billed |
| Property tax | Owner | As billed |
| Electricity | Tenant | On meter reading |
| Gas and internet | Tenant | On actual bills |
| Late fee or penalty on dues | Whoever caused the delay | Written in clause |

Add one more line: when a bill is raised, the tenant gets a copy of it, and the owner gets a copy of any payment receipt. ## Late fees and interest

The Rules cap interest on overdue society dues at 12% simple interest a year. If a late payment by the tenant leads to interest, the tenant should bear it. If the owner delays, the owner should. Say so.
