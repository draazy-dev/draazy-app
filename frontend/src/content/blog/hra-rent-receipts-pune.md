To claim HRA on rent paid in Pune, give your employer a rent receipt for each period, plus your landlord's PAN if you pay more than ₹1 lakh a year in rent. HRA is only available under the old tax regime. If you pay rent by bank transfer, your statement is the strongest proof, and the receipt backs it up.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general information, not tax advice. Rules changed on 1 April 2026 with the new Income-tax Act, 2025. Check with your employer's payroll team or a chartered accountant before you file.

## Who can claim HRA?

You can claim HRA if you are salaried, your pay includes an HRA component, you pay rent for the home you live in, and you choose the old tax regime. If you choose the new regime, there is no HRA exemption, whatever your rent.

The exemption is the lowest of three amounts: the HRA your employer pays, your rent minus 10% of salary, and a share of salary. That share is 50% in the metro list and 40% elsewhere. From 1 April 2026 the 50% list grew from four cities to eight, and Pune is now on it. Your payroll team's HRA calculator will do the sums.

## What should a rent receipt include?

A receipt does not need a fixed format. Include these details so that it stands up to a payroll check:

- Tenant's full name and the landlord's full name
- Full address of the flat
- Period the rent covers, for example "1 to 30 November 2026"
- Amount paid, in figures and words
- Date and mode of payment: bank transfer, UPI, cheque or cash
- Landlord's PAN, when it is required
- Landlord's signature, and a revenue stamp if the rent is paid in cash

## When do you need the landlord's PAN?

If the rent you pay one landlord is more than ₹1 lakh in a financial year, your employer needs that landlord's PAN. A ₹10,000 monthly rent comes to ₹1,20,000 a year, so the rule applies to most Pune rentals.

If the landlord has no PAN, they sign a declaration giving their name and address and stating that they have no PAN. Keep a copy.

From the 2026-27 financial year, you are also expected to state how you are related to your landlord if you claim HRA, for example if you pay rent to a parent or spouse. Paying rent to a relative is allowed, but the rent must really be paid, by bank transfer, and the landlord should show it as income in their own return.

## Do you need a revenue stamp?

For cash payments, receipts above ₹5,000 are customarily stamped with a ₹1 revenue stamp, which the landlord signs across. You do not need one for payments by bank transfer, UPI or cheque, because the bank record already proves the payment.

The simplest habit is to pay rent from your own account to the landlord's account. There is then nothing to stamp and nothing to argue about.

## Does the landlord have to give a receipt?

Yes. Section 31 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 says every landlord must give a written receipt for any amount received in respect of the premises, when the amount is received. Failing to do so can attract a fine of up to ₹100 for each day of default.

Ask for a receipt for the deposit as well, not only for rent. Our [security deposit guide](/blog/security-deposit-pune) covers what to write down.

## What else should you keep?

Receipts are one part of the file. Keep these for at least the period in which your return can be reopened:

- A copy of your registered [rent agreement](/blog/rent-agreement-pune)
- Bank statements showing each rent payment
- Landlord's PAN or the no-PAN declaration
- The HRA declaration you gave your employer

If the receipts and the bank entries do not match, for example the amounts differ or the payee is someone other than the landlord, a claim can be questioned.
