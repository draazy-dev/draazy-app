The fairest way to split rent is by what each person gets: the room's size, whether it has an attached bathroom or balcony, and whether it is the only room with a cupboard or air-conditioner. Split shared bills equally, unless one person clearly uses more. Decide all of it before moving in, write it down and agree how deposits come back when someone leaves.

| Item | Suggested approach | Basis |

## How do you split rent fairly?

There are three common methods. Pick the one that fits your flat.

### Equal split

Every flatmate pays the same. This works when rooms are similar in size and facilities. It is simple and rarely causes arguments.

### Split by room size

If one room is clearly larger, that person pays more. Work it out like this: add the area of the bedrooms (measure in square feet), and share the total rent in proportion. Shared spaces such as the hall and kitchen are usually treated as equal and left out of the weighting.

### Split with extras

Add a premium for features: an attached bathroom, a balcony, a built-in wardrobe or an air-conditioner. A simple way is to set a base share, then agree a fixed amount for each extra.

### A worked example

Three flatmates share a 3 BHK for ₹45,000 a month. Rooms: one master bedroom with an attached bathroom and balcony, and two smaller bedrooms that share one bathroom. Taking the master room as worth ₹1,500 a month more than a smaller room:

| Flatmate | Room | Share |
|---|---|---|
| A | Master with attached bath and balcony | ₹16,000 |
| B | Smaller room, shared bath | ₹14,500 |
| C | Smaller room, shared bath | ₹14,500 |
| **Total** | | **₹45,000** |

## How do you split the bills?

Bills fall into groups. Treat each differently:

- **Electricity.** The flat usually has one MSEDCL meter. Split it equally, or give an air-conditioned room a larger share. Photograph the bill, and pay from a shared account if you can.
- **Piped gas, water and society maintenance.** Split equally, since everyone uses the kitchen and bathrooms. See [who pays maintenance](/blog/maintenance-charges-who-pays-pune).
- **Wi-Fi and streaming.** Equal split. Whoever holds the account owns the router.
- **Cook, maid or cleaner.** Equal split, unless someone opts out of the service.
- **Groceries and supplies.** Keep these separate, or run a small shared kitty with a monthly settlement.

An app or a shared spreadsheet is enough to track who paid what. Settle on one fixed day every month, such as the 5th, and do not let balances run for months.

## Who pays the owner?

Pick one person to pay the owner by bank transfer, with others transferring their share to that person by the same day. A single payment makes the owner's records clean. Have the owner issue the rent receipt in the paying person's name, and make sure the others can see it. If someone needs the receipt for tax, our guide to [HRA rent receipts](/blog/hra-rent-receipts-pune) explains what to include. If you each claim HRA, each of you needs proof of your own share, so keep your transfer records.

## How should the deposit be handled?

A flat's deposit can be several months' rent, so each flatmate's share is large. Manage it carefully:

1. **Each person pays their share by bank transfer** to whoever pays the owner, with a note saying what it is.
2. **Record each share in the written understanding.** Name, amount and date.
3. **Agree what happens when someone leaves.** The usual rule is that the leaving flatmate gets their share back when the replacement pays theirs, or when the owner returns the deposit.
4. **Agree deductions for damage up front.** Only damage a person or their guests caused is deducted from their share.

Read our [security deposit guide](/blog/security-deposit-pune) for the refund timeline and what an owner may deduct.

## What if someone leaves in the middle of a month?

Agree a notice period, for example 30 days, in the understanding. The leaving person pays their rent and bills until the notice ends, or until a replacement joins. They also pay their share of any bills that arrive after they leave, for the days they lived there.

The new person pays their own share from the day they move in. They should not inherit unpaid bills from before.

## When do disagreements start?

Mostly with unclear arrangements: electricity in summer, a guest who stays for weeks, a deposit that never comes back. A one-page note covers most of them. Find flatmates through [Draazy Flatmates](/flatmates), meet them before you commit, and settle the split before you pay a rupee.
