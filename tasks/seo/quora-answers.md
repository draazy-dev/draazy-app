## 1. How can I rent a flat in Pune without a broker?

You can, and the main risk is not the lack of a broker but the lack of any check on the person you are dealing with. A workable way:

1. **Look where owners post directly.** Society notice boards and "To Let" boards, the security guard or society office, friends and colleagues who are leaving a flat, and property sites that have an owner filter. Treat the "owner" tag with caution: some brokers use it.
2. **Visit before you discuss money.** See the flat, check that the keys work, look at the water supply, the electricity meter and the parking.
3. **Meet the owner, not only a "caretaker".** Match the name on the ID to the name on the property tax bill or the society maintenance receipt. A genuine owner will not mind.
4. **Negotiate the terms yourself.** Rent, deposit (two to three months is common in Pune, and it is negotiable), lock-in, notice period.
5. **Sign a registered leave and licence agreement.** In Maharashtra a notarised agreement is not enough. The owner has to register it, and you can do it online.
6. **Pay by bank transfer to the owner's account,** never cash, and never a token before you have seen the flat.

Doing all this saves the brokerage, which is commonly 15 days' to a month's rent, but it costs you some time. If you want to see how a site can check owners before you contact them, here is how we do it at Draazy: https://draazy.com/how-verification-works

## 2. How much security deposit is normal for a flat in Pune?

There is no fixed rule. For flats in Pune, **two to three months' rent** is common, and some owners in premium societies ask for more. Furnished flats usually carry a higher deposit than unfurnished ones because there is more to repair.

**Is there a legal cap?** No, not in Pune at the moment. You may have read that deposits are capped at two months. That comes from the central government's Model Tenancy Act, 2021, which is only a template that states can adopt. As far as I know, Maharashtra has not adopted it, and the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 does not cap the deposit. Be careful with social media posts that say otherwise.

**It is negotiable.** A steady job, references and a longer lock-in help you bring it down.

**Protect yourself with the agreement.** It should state:
- the amount and how it was paid (use bank transfer, not cash),
- the refund timeline, for example 7 or 15 days after you return the keys,
- what can be deducted: unpaid rent, unpaid bills and damage beyond normal wear and tear,
- that ordinary wear and tear is not deducted,
- the notice period and what happens if either side leaves early.

On day one, make an inventory with the owner and take dated photos of every room. Both of you should sign it. It decides most disputes later.

## 3. Is it compulsory to register a rent agreement in Maharashtra?

Yes. In Maharashtra a rental agreement is a **leave and licence agreement**, and Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 says it must be in writing and **registered**. That includes the usual 11-month agreement. In many other states, agreements under 11 months can be left unregistered, but that is not true here.

**Who has to register?** The duty is on the landlord (the licensor). Not registering can attract a penalty: imprisonment up to three months, a fine up to ₹5,000, or both.

**Notarised is not registered.** An agreement on stamp paper that has only been notarised does not meet the requirement.

**Why it matters to a tenant too:** if there is no registered agreement and a dispute arises, the terms the tenant states are presumed true unless the landlord can prove otherwise. So registration protects both sides.

**What you need:**
- Owner and tenant: Aadhaar and PAN.
- Owner: proof of ownership (Index II, sale deed or a recent property tax bill).
- Two witnesses with Aadhaar. Witnesses also give biometric verification.

Maharashtra allows online registration, so nobody has to go to the sub-registrar's office. Confirm the current process and fees on the IGR Maharashtra website, and talk to a lawyer for anything unusual. This is general information, not legal advice.

## 4. How much is the stamp duty and registration fee for a rent agreement in Pune?

For a leave and licence agreement in Maharashtra, stamp duty is **0.25%** of the agreement's value. The value is:

1. **Rent for the whole term:** monthly rent × number of months, plus any non-refundable deposit, and
2. **Notional interest on the refundable deposit:** 10% a year, for each year or part of a year of the term.

**Example (2 BHK in Wakad, 11 months):**
- Rent ₹25,000 × 11 = ₹2,75,000
- Refundable deposit ₹1,00,000, so notional interest is ₹10,000
- Agreement value = ₹2,85,000
- Stamp duty at 0.25% = **₹712.50**

On top of that there is a **registration fee**: ₹1,000 for property within municipal limits (PMC and PCMC) and ₹500 in rural areas. An online or doorstep service will add its own service fee, which is separate from government charges.

Notice that a bigger refundable deposit slightly raises the stamp duty, because of the notional interest.

The exact amount is worked out on the IGR Maharashtra website. Fees and rules change by government notification, so check the current figures before you pay. If you want to try your own numbers quickly, we are building a free calculator: https://draazy.com/tools/rent-agreement-cost-pune

## 5. How do I claim HRA if my landlord does not give rent receipts?

Short answer: ask for receipts, or make them yourself and get the landlord to sign. Employers usually want them for HRA proof, and they are easy to produce.

**What most employers ask for:**
- Rent receipts for the period, with the landlord's name and address, the amount, the period and the landlord's signature.
- The landlord's **PAN**, usually when the rent you pay is more than ₹1,00,000 in a year.
- A copy of the rent agreement, in many cases.

**Practical advice:**
- Pay rent by bank transfer or UPI to the landlord's account, so there is a record. Your bank statement shows the payment.
- Ask your payroll team exactly what they accept. Requirements differ by company, and I am not a tax professional. For personal tax advice talk to a CA.
- If the landlord has no PAN or will not share it, tell your payroll team early. They will say what declaration they accept.

## 6. How do I avoid rental scams in Pune?

Most rental scams in Pune follow one script: a flat that looks too good for the price, an owner who cannot meet you, and a request for money before you have seen anything. The amounts are small (a ₹5,000 token or a ₹10,000 "booking charge"), which is why people pay without thinking.

**Common patterns**
- An "owner" who is out of town (often claiming army or government posting) and promises to courier the keys after you pay.
- An urgent token: "three families are interested, pay today".
- A "refundable" gate-pass or visit fee. It never comes back, and the next demand is bigger.
- A QR code sent to "receive a refund". Scanning a QR and entering your UPI PIN only takes money out of your account.
- A photo switch: the listing shows a nice flat, and on site you are shown a worse, costlier one.

**Checks that protect you**
1. **Never pay before you have seen the flat and met the owner.**
2. Speak to the owner on the day of the visit and match their name with their ID.
3. Ask for the Index II or sale deed, or at least a recent property tax or society maintenance bill in the same name.
4. Confirm with the society office that the person has the right to rent it out.
5. Pay by bank transfer to the owner's account only after the agreement is ready.

If you have been scammed, report it at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 immediately.

## 7. PG or shared flat in Pune: which is better for a fresher?

Neither is better for everyone. It depends on budget, how long you will stay and how much you want to manage yourself.

**PG (paying guest)**
- Quickest to start: small deposit, one monthly amount that often covers food, housekeeping, Wi-Fi and electricity.
- Good for freshers, students and short assignments, or while you learn which area suits you.
- Watch for house rules (guests, entry times, cooking), two or three people in one room, hidden charges for laundry or electricity, and unclear notice and deposit refund terms. Get everything in writing.

**Shared flat**
- More space and privacy, your own room, a kitchen, no house rules beyond the society's.
- Often cheaper per person than a good PG in a group of three or four.
- But there is a bigger security deposit (split between flatmates), shared bills to agree on, and the question of what happens when someone leaves.
- Ask that every flatmate is named in the leave and licence agreement.

**Two things that apply to both:** police verification (owners and PG operators in Pune and Pimpri Chinchwad must share resident details with the police), and the rule never to pay a token for a place you have not seen.

A rough rule: choose a PG for the first few months, move into a flat share when you know the city and have people to share with.

## 8. Hinjawadi, Wakad or Baner: where should I live if I work in Hinjawadi IT Park?

It comes down to commute against lifestyle.

- **Hinjawadi (including Maan and Marunji):** shortest commute and generally lower rents. Fewer restaurants, malls and hospitals, although that is improving. In some societies the water supply depends partly on tankers, so ask before you sign. Best for freshers and flatmates who want to save time and money.
- **Wakad:** right next to Hinjawadi Phase 1, in Pimpri Chinchwad limits. Mid-range rents, a large choice of gated societies, schools, hospitals and a big mall. Traffic near the Wakad bridge and highway junctions at peak hours is the main downside. Rent varies sharply between societies. Best for families and working couples.
- **Baner (with Balewadi):** within Pune city limits, with the best choice of cafés, restaurants and gyms. Rents and deposits are higher, and the drive to Hinjawadi can be long at peak hours.

**Metro Line 3** will connect Hinjawadi and Shivajinagar through Balewadi and Baner. As of early October 2026 passenger service had not started, so do not plan your commute around it yet.

**Test before you sign:** do the commute at your real office time at least once; visit in the evening; ask the society about water, power backup and parking; and talk to a resident or the guard.

## 9. How do I check whether a project in Pune is RERA registered?

Use the MahaRERA website (maharera.maharashtra.gov.in). It is free, and in about ten minutes it tells you whether the project is registered, who the promoter is, when the flat is due and whether work is on track.

**Does a project need to be registered?** A project must be registered with MahaRERA before it can be advertised or sold if the land is larger than 500 square metres, or if it has more than eight flats across all phases. Most housing projects in Pune fall in this net.

**Find the registration number.** It begins with **P** followed by 11 digits, for example P52100012345. You will find it on the brochure, hoardings and website, and in every advertisement. Since August 2023 MahaRERA has required a QR code next to it on all ads.

**What to check on the project page**
- The registration is valid and the project name and promoter match what you were told.
- The declared completion date, and whether it has been extended.
- Progress reports and any complaints.
- The same registration number on the agreement for sale.

If a seller cannot give you a registration number for a project that should have one, treat that as a red flag. This is general information, not legal advice; have a lawyer read the documents before you pay.

## 10. Is police verification of tenants mandatory in Pune?

Yes. Pune City Police issue a standing order under **Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**, which replaced Section 144 of the old CrPC. It requires owners, and agents who arrange rentals, to submit tenant details to the police. The order is reissued every couple of months, so it is always in force. The same applies to PG accommodation and to flats in Pimpri Chinchwad, which has its own police commissionerate.

**Penalty:** owners who skip it can face action under Section 223 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for disobeying a public servant's order: a fine, imprisonment or both. Pune Police acted against many landlords and PG operators in 2026 after checking drives.

**How:**
- Pune City: the tenant or PG information form on the Pune City Police website (punepolice.gov.in).
- Pimpri Chinchwad (Wakad, Hinjawadi, Pimple Saudagar, Ravet and so on): the tenant information page at pcpc.gov.in/tenant-info, or at the local police station or by registered post.

**Keep ready:** the owner's ID and contact details, the tenant's photo, Aadhaar or other photo ID, permanent address and workplace or college details, and a copy of the registered agreement.

You do **not** need a police NOC to rent out a flat; you only inform the police. Do it before or on the day the tenant moves in, and again when a new tenant or flatmate arrives. Registering the agreement online shares details with the police, but they still advise owners to submit directly.
